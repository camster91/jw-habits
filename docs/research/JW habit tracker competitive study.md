# Forgiving Streaks Open an Unclaimed Witness Niche

The best-documented ways to make a habit tracker simple and fun are not points, pets or leaderboards. They are **one-tap check-ins, a low bar for what counts, built-in grace days, free streak repair, and a brief, satisfying moment of celebration**. Peer-reviewed work shows that slack and repair keep people going after a miss, and that a broken streak hurts most when people blame themselves. Duolingo's biggest published streak gains came from making the streak easier to keep, not from adding pressure. For Jehovah's Witnesses, no maintained app combines the daily text, Bible reading, meeting preparation, family worship, personal study and a ministry check-in that fits the post-November 2023 reporting rules into one gentle checklist. JW Library supplies the content but tracks nothing. Reading-plan apps cover only reading. Ministry apps are mostly hour timers. The one app that once offered reminders for daily text, family worship and personal study was last updated in 2018. The rebuilt app's main risks are in branding, not in the product. "JW" in the name or icon is the costliest word available. Watch Tower asserts it as a mark and has litigated over it, and both Apple and Google bar names and icons that imply affiliation. jw.org's terms also forbid bundling its text or artwork inside any app. The winning design is therefore a neutral brand that runs fully on the device, is clearly native rather than a web wrapper, links out to JW Library for content, and frames every miss as normal.

## Three camps, and only two apps pair privacy with non-daily schedules

The habit-tracker market falls into three camps. **Minimal trackers that respect your data** (Loop, Streaks, Way of Life) win on low friction and a one-time or zero price. **Cloud productivity trackers sold by subscription** (Habitify, Productive) add analytics, integrations and paywalls. **Gamified trackers** (Habitica, Finch, (Not Boring) Habits) get their fun from a layer added on top of the habits: an RPG, a pet, or tactile 3D check-ins. Only Loop and Streaks combine no-account privacy with flexible non-daily schedules. That combination matters most here, because Witness routines mix daily items (daily text, reading) with fixed-weekday items (two meetings, family worship) and a monthly one (the ministry report).

Several cells for Way of Life, Habitica and Finch rest on third-party reviews, because official pages blocked the fetcher. Exact check-in gestures (tap or long-press) for Loop, Habitify and Way of Life could not be confirmed.

Two design ideas stand out. **Loop's strength score** is a weighted average over every repetition since day one, with recent ones counting more, so a strong habit "recovers quickly from brief lapses" rather than resetting. A perfect daily habit reaches 80% in about a month. A weekly habit takes about **seven months** to reach the same level, though, which would feel glacial for a meetings or family-worship item ([Loop discussion #689](https://github.com/iSoron/uhabits/discussions/689)). **Streaks' schedule model** (specific weekdays, X days per week, every N days) is the closest fit to fixed-day congregation events ([Streaks](https://streaksapp.com/)). The synthesis for this app is to schedule like Streaks, and then count weekly items by occurrence ("8 of the last 8 weeks") instead of letting a calendar-day chain or a slow-converging score punish them.

User sentiment is consistent but thinly sourced. Roundups that summarize Reddit discussion say users favor "simple streak tracking, low-friction checklists, and visible progress." Those same sources name the reasons people quit as logging friction, feature overload, gamification novelty wearing off, and rigid daily requirements ([Recurrr](https://recurrr.com/articles/best-habit-tracker-reddit)). The most-cited complaint is that "one slip, the streak resets to zero, and the app quietly becomes a source of guilt" ([Together with Kai](https://togetherwithkai.com/blog/best-habit-tracker-apps)). Both are vendor blogs that cite no threads, so treat the "most users quit within two weeks" claim as directional. The behavioral research below, however, points the same way.

## Fun comes from forgiveness and feel, not from points

The strongest evidence on streaks shows that they work when they are **easy to keep and survivable when broken**. In 2020 Duolingo separated "extend the streak" from "hit your daily goal", so one lesson keeps the streak alive. The result was **+10.5% daily learners on a streak (+19% for new learners) and +3.3% Day-14 retention** ([Duolingo, 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Letting users hold two Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by 0.38%, and new streak animations raised 7-day return by 1.7% ([Duolingo, 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). These are company figures chosen by the company, not peer-reviewed results. Peer-reviewed work supports the same direction. In Silverman & Barasch's seven studies, a log that shows an intact streak raises later engagement *regardless of actual past behavior*. The drop after a broken streak is **amplified by self-blame and softened when repair is possible** ([CU Boulder summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). Sharif & Shu found that goals with a few "emergency reserve" skip days produced **47–55% rebound after a failure versus 37%** for hard goals with no slack. Reserve groups hit their goals up to 40% more often ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/)).

The habit-formation research also argues against all-or-nothing chains. Lally's study put the median time to automaticity at **66 days, with a range of 18 to 254**, and the author warns against quoting that number out of context ([University of Surrey](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally)). The same paper reports that missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation ([Lally et al. 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674)). That wording comes from the abstract and was not re-verified in this pass. The best-evidenced lever is planning: implementation intentions ("after X, I will Y") show **d = 0.65** across 94 tests ([summary of Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/implementation-intentions)). Stawarz et al. reviewed 115 habit apps and ran a 4-week study. They found that reminders supported repetition but **hindered automaticity**, that cues tied to existing routines helped, and that positive reinforcement had no measurable effect ([UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/)).

Gamification itself has a real but modest effect: Hedges g = 0.42 during interventions, falling to 0.15 at follow-up, across 16 RCTs ([Mazeas et al., JMIR 2022](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/e26779)). The ingredients matter. A 128-study meta-analysis found that expected, tangible, contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation (d from −0.28 to −0.40), while positive informational feedback *enhances* it ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)). In devotional practice the intrinsic motive is the whole point, so an economy of points for reading is the wrong kind of fun. The right kind is the tactile check-in that (Not Boring) Habits made famous (hold, haptic, animation) and progress you can see. The family-team BE FIT trial is a useful template: cooperative family goals with **5 "lifelines"** for sick days raised goal achievement from 0.32 to 0.53 of days, and part of the gain persisted after the game ended ([Patel et al., JAMA Intern Med 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710273)).

### Twelve patterns worth adopting

Evidence strength: **Strong** means a peer-reviewed study or meta-analysis. **Moderate** means first-party company data or a clear product precedent. **Weak** means user reviews, roundups or practitioner opinion.

| # | Pattern | Why | Evidence and strength | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One-tap check-in from the list and a home-screen widget** | Logging friction is a top reason people quit; Loop and Streaks are praised for "open it, tap, move on" | [Loop](https://github.com/iSoron/uhabits), [Streaks](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/streaks/id963034692), [Recurrr](https://recurrr.com/articles/best-habit-tracker-reddit). **Weak–Moderate** | The Today screen is the app. A widget also strengthens Apple's 4.2 "app-like" case |
| 2 | **Lower the bar: the smallest act counts** | Duolingo's largest streak lift came from "one lesson keeps the streak" | [Duolingo 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak): +10.5% on streak, +3.3% D14. **Moderate** | One chapter, or listening to a reading, counts as "read today"; partial days are allowed (users of NWT Reading praise this) |
| 3 | **Built-in grace days that apply automatically** | Slack raises persistence after a miss; freezes raised DAU | [Sharif & Shu](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/) 47–55% vs 37% rebound; [Duolingo 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit); [BE FIT lifelines](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710273). **Strong** | For example, 2 grace days per month per routine, applied silently and never sold |
| 4 | **Free repair / catch-up** | Repair softens the drop in engagement after a break | [Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions). **Strong** | Reading two chapters tomorrow fills in yesterday |
| 5 | **Schedule-aware streaks counted by occurrence** | Fixed-day routines should not be punished on off days; slack research favors weekly framing | [Streaks schedules](https://streaksapp.com/); [Loop weekly convergence](https://github.com/iSoron/uhabits/discussions/689). **Moderate** (no head-to-head study exists) | Meetings on the congregation's two set days; family worship weekly; ministry monthly |
| 6 | **Cumulative progress at least as prominent as any consecutive counter** | How the log *represents* behavior drives engagement; one miss does not undo a habit | [Silverman & Barasch](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); [Lally](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674). **Strong** | "142 days of reading this year", a calendar that fills in, a Loop-style strength ring for daily items |
| 7 | **Implementation-intention onboarding tied to an existing routine** | The best-evidenced behavior-change lever; event cues build automaticity | [Gollwitzer & Sheeran](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/implementation-intentions) d=0.65; [Stawarz](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/). **Strong** | "When will you consider the daily text? After breakfast / with family prayer / before bed" |
| 8 | **One reminder at the chosen anchor time, worded as an invitation, optionally fading** | Reminders give a small real lift, but relying on them hinders automaticity | [Bidargaddi 2018](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/11/e10123) +3.9% engagement; [Stawarz](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/). **Strong** for the lift; fading is **untested** | Local notifications only; "After breakfast, today's text is ready" |
| 9 | **A brief tactile celebration on check-in** | The cheapest source of "fun"; animations measurably helped Duolingo | [Not Boring](https://www.tapsmart.com/apps/review-not-boring-habits/); [Duolingo +1.7%](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit). **Moderate**; Stawarz found reinforcement did not improve automaticity, so keep it light | Haptic plus a soft animation; no points |
| 10 | **Milestones and informational feedback** | Positive informational feedback enhances intrinsic motivation | [Deci et al. 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf). **Strong** | A Bible-book map filling in, "12 of 66 books", finishing a whole reading of the Bible |
| 11 | **Fresh-start prompts at landmarks** | People restart aspirational behavior at the start of a week, month or year | [Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf). **Strong** (effect sizes not extracted) | "New week" on Monday; new month alongside the ministry check-in; new service year in September |
| 12 | **A cooperative family goal with lifelines and no rankings** | Family-team accountability worked in an RCT | [BE FIT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710273). **Strong** for the structure; **untested** for devotional use and with BE FIT's loss framing removed | "Family worship: 4 of the last 4 weeks", kept on one shared device, since the app has no accounts |

A thirteenth idea is weaker and better treated as a default than as a pattern: start with a small suggested set of routines. Streaks' cap is seen as protection against burnout ([Rebooting](https://rebooting.substack.com/p/cant-habit-all)), and Witness routines map naturally to about six items.

## What to avoid: guilt engines, reward economies and paywalls

Every anti-pattern below appears in a successful mainstream app. Each conflicts with either the evidence above or the culture of this audience. Duolingo openly says it relies on **loss aversion** once a long streak's novelty fades ([Duolingo 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). That works for language learning, but it is exactly where "motivating" turns into "anxious obligation" for a devotional routine. Witness culture also moved away from quantitative pressure in November 2023, when hour reporting was dropped for publishers and a Governing Body member said "our ministry involves much more than counting time" ([NBC News / RNS](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/religion/timekeepers-no-rank-file-jehovahs-witnesses-say-goodbye-tracking-prose-rcna126582)).

| Anti-pattern | Seen in | Why to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Hard reset to zero with "You broke your streak" copy | Streaks ([site](https://streaksapp.com/)) | Self-blame amplifies disengagement ([Silverman & Barasch](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)); it is the top quit reason in reviews ([Together with Kai](https://togetherwithkai.com/blog/best-habit-tracker-apps)) |
| Escalating, loss-framed nags ("your streak is about to die") | Duolingo ([blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)) | Reminders substitute for automaticity ([Stawarz](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/)); they jar with the tone of a spiritual routine |
| Paid freezes or paid repair | Duolingo gems ([wiki](https://duolingo.fandom.com/wiki/Shop/Streak_freeze)) | Charging for grace in a spiritual context undermines trust; repair works best when it is free |
| Points, currency or loot for reading | Habitica, Finch stones ([WhistleOut](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Apps/finch-self-care-app-review)) | Expected tangible rewards undermine intrinsic motivation ([Deci et al.](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)) |
| Punitive companion or party damage | Habitica ([wiki](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Rest_in_the_Inn)) | Turns one person's miss into shame for others; Finch users credit the *absence* of punishment for sticking with it ([Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/finch-app-reviews-emotional-attachment-user-retention-product-loyalty/)) |
| Leaderboards and rankings inside a family | Productive ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/productive-habit-tracker/id983826477)) | Comparing people clashes with the devotional motive; BE FIT worked with *team* framing |
| An hour timer as the centerpiece of ministry | Most ministry apps ([jwministryapp.com](http://www.jwministryapp.com/en/)) | Since Nov 2023 most publishers report only participation and Bible studies; hours matter mainly to pioneers |
| Subscriptions, weekly plans, ads or habit-count paywalls | Copycat "JW Library – My Holy Bible App" at $7.99/week ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760021268)); Habitify's 3-habit free tier | Witness users read monetization as a red flag for unofficial or deceptive apps ([Library Online reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/watchtower-library-2024/id1436849154)) |
| Feature bloat and social layers | Habitica, Productive | Named as reasons people quit ([Recurrr](https://recurrr.com/articles/best-habit-tracker-reddit)); social layers would also need accounts this app does not have |
| Embedding jw.org text, the daily text or artwork | Unofficial content apps | Forbidden by the [jw.org Terms of Use](https://www.jw.org/en/terms-of-use/) and Apple 5.2.2 |

## No maintained app ties together the whole Witness week

**JW Library** (rated 4.8 from about 48K ratings) carries the Bible, the daily text, midweek and weekend meeting materials, notes and tags. Its release notes from 2024 to 2026 show no reminders, streaks, reading plans or habit features ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jw-library/id672417831)). The only built-in tracking is check boxes inside specific publications, such as the *Enjoy Life Forever!* "Track Your Bible Reading" chart ([jw.org](https://www.jw.org/en/news/region/global/Use-Enjoy-Life-Forever-More-Effectively-With-Features-in-JW-Library)). Its 2025–2026 roadmap (redesigned Meetings tab, multi-window, shuffle) is about content and media, so a habit companion is unlikely to be made redundant soon. That last point is an inference, not a known plan.

Third-party apps cover the rest only in pieces. **NWT Reading** is free and open source, rated 4.5 from 54 ratings. It offers multiple reading plans, shows how far ahead or behind you are, and opens passages in JW Library. A reviewer was frustrated when an update moved their target date, and another praised being able to log a partial day's reading ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1452909600)). **Ministry apps** such as Field Service Report Pro, Ministry Report, Service Reports+ and JW Ministry+ center on timers, return visits, territories and Bible studies ([jwministryapp.com](http://www.jwministryapp.com/en/); [JW Ministry+](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756040136)). Yet the November 2023 change means most publishers now report only **whether they shared in the ministry plus a Bible-study count**, while pioneers still report hours ([RNS](https://religionnews.com/2023/11/27/timekeepers-no-more-rank-and-file-jehovahs-witnesses-say-goodbye-to-tracking-proselytizing-hours/)). The closest thing to the target product is **Bible Study Tool**. It has 743 ratings at 4.8, four reading lists including Family Worship, and customizable reminders for the daily text, family worship and personal study, but its last listed update is August 2018 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id734412227)). Its reviews and a JWTalk thread ask for tracking several reading programs at once and for planning study weeks ahead. In that thread, users pieced it together with printed PDFs and TickTick ([JWTalk](https://jwtalk.net/topic/38750-app-request-bible-reading-tracking/)).

The opportunity is a single gentle checklist shaped to the Witness week:

| Routine | Cadence | Check-in model | Link-out (no embedded content) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily text | Daily | One tap; counts even if read late in the day | Open JW Library / jw.org |
| Bible reading | Daily or X days/week | Chapter-level progress against a chosen plan; partial days count; free catch-up | jw.org finder link per chapter |
| Meeting preparation | The congregation's 2 set weekdays (user-set) | Prompt a day or two before; counted per meeting | JW Library's Meetings tab |
| Family worship | Weekly | Shared family item with lifelines | — |
| Personal study | X times/week | Flexible-day weekly target | — |
| Ministry | Monthly | "Shared in the ministry this month" toggle plus Bible-study count; optional hours mode for pioneers | — |

Linking is technically proven. Third-party apps already open JW Library through jw.org **finder universal links**, for example `https://www.jw.org/finder?wtlocale=E&prefer=lang&bible=19083018&pub=nwtsty` with the reference encoded as book/chapter/verse ([JW MCP README](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@advenimus/jw-mcp/blob/a847af6594a8a73fb4a6b2786e8565ab5fa9d146/README.md)), and through a community-documented `jwlibrary://` scheme ([Obsidian JW Library Linker](https://www.obsidianstats.com/plugins/jw-library-linker)). Neither has official documentation, and jw.org's terms neither permit nor forbid deep linking explicitly, so the finder universal link is the safer default because it falls back to the web. Expectations about price are set by the category: NWT Reading, Bible Study Tool and JW Ministry+ are free with no in-app purchases, and **JW Ministry+ already markets local-only storage** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756040136)). An on-device, no-account design is therefore a selling point, not a limitation. The one cost is that family features must work on a shared device or a single household profile, because there is no server to sync family members. None of these opportunity hypotheses has been tested with users, and store searches may have missed a 2025–2026 entrant. A manual search for "daily text", "theocratic" and "spiritual routine" should come before launch.

## "JW" in the name is the costliest word you could choose

Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society of Pennsylvania holds **JW.ORG as a US registration (No. 4797582)** ([Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/858/96/jw-85896124.html)). It also holds a Canadian JW.ORG logo registration that explicitly covers **"mobile applications"** ([CIPO](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/cipo/trademark-search/1754140)). It asserted common-law use of "JW" since 1931 when it petitioned to cancel the "JW PLAYER" marks, and the case survived a motion to dismiss ([SSJR](https://www.ssjr.com/articles/view/238)). The final outcome was not found, and the US status of plain "JW" or "JW LIBRARY" as word marks is unverified. Separately, the jw.org Terms of Use forbid distributing its "artwork, electronic publications, trademarks, music, photos, text, or videos ... with or as part of any software application." They also forbid scraping tools, with a narrow exception for free apps that download EPUB/PDF/MP3/MP4 files. Sharing links is allowed ([jw.org Terms](https://www.jw.org/en/terms-of-use/); [JW Library terms](https://www.jw.org/en/terms-of-use-jwlibrary/)).

The store rules turn that into concrete constraints. On Apple, **4.1(c)** bars another party's brand or product name in the app's name or icon without approval. **5.2.1** bars protected trademarks and "copycat representations" in the name, metadata or developer name. **2.3.7** limits the name to 30 characters, bans packing metadata with trademarked terms, and says subtitles should not reference other apps. **5.2.2** requires permission to display a third-party service's content, which together with the jw.org terms rules out showing the daily text in-app. **4.2.3(i)** requires the app to work without another app installed, so it must stand on its own and not simply launch JW Library. **1.1.1** bars mean-spirited religious commentary, which matters for any copy about other faiths or former members ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google Play's **Impersonation** policy bars names, icons and developer names that "falsely imply a relationship to or authorization by someone" ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374?hl=en)). Its **IP** policy allows suspension for confusingly similar marks ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888072?hl=en)). Its **Metadata** policy caps titles at 30 characters and bans emoji, repeated special characters and unauthorized logos ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)). Wrapping wol.jw.org in a WebView would breach Play's Spam policy as well as the jw.org terms ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)).

| Placement | Risk | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| jw.org logo, a blue-square look-alike icon, or "JW Library"-style naming | Very high | Never; these are registered or claimed marks that cover mobile apps |
| "JW" / "JW.ORG" in the app name or developer name | High | Avoid; this is what 4.1(c), 5.2.1 and Play Impersonation target |
| "JW" in the iOS subtitle or Play short description | High–moderate | Avoid (2.3.7 subtitle rule) |
| "Jehovah's Witnesses" as a single iOS keyword | Moderate | Acceptable sparingly; Apple may strip it |
| "for Jehovah's Witnesses" in the long description, with a disclaimer | Lowest | The recommended way to be found; it describes a religion, not a product (a legal judgment counsel should confirm) |

Unofficial LDS apps show the disclaimer pattern, for example "not affiliated with or endorsed by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints" ([Temple Appointment Scheduling](https://apps.apple.com/hn/app/temple-appointment-scheduling/id1630788532)). "LDS" is a nickname the church does not appear to enforce against apps, whereas "JW" is actively asserted, so that precedent does not carry over to naming. A disclaimer helps but does not cure a confusing name. The one live unofficial JW-content app, "Library Online 2026", subtitled "Watchtower & Daily Text", says it is unofficial. Users still call it "designed to look like it comes from the brothers" and flag its personal-name developer and paid ad-free upsell ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/watchtower-library-2024/id1436849154)). A suitable disclaimer would read: "*[App] is an independent app. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society or jw.org, and contains no content from jw.org.*"

Two constraints are specific to Capacitor apps. Apple rejected a Capacitor app at least twice under **4.2** in January 2026, despite native location, share-sheet and Maps features, as not different enough from browsing the web ([Apple forum 812889](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889)). A habit tracker is on stronger ground. It should bundle all web assets locally (no remote `server.url`) and use **local notifications, haptics and a native WidgetKit / Android widget**. These native pieces should be visible from the first screen and described in plain language in the review notes. On compliance, both stores require a **privacy policy URL** (Apple 5.1.1(i), also linked inside the app) and a support contact (1.5). Google requires a **Data safety form** even from apps that collect nothing, and data processed only on the device need not be disclosed ([Play Data safety](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en)). Apple's "Data Not Collected" label should fit, but its exact definition was not verified here, and any crash or analytics SDK would change the answer. New personal Play developer accounts must run a **closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days** before Production unlocks. Organization accounts are exempt ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)).

## Conclusion

The research inverts the usual habit-app playbook for this audience. The features that make mainstream apps "sticky" (loss-aversion streaks, reward currencies, paywalled caps, social comparison) are the ones most likely to backfire with a devotional audience whose own organization stepped back from counting time in 2023. The features with the best evidence (grace days, free repair, cumulative progress, routine-anchored planning, informational milestones) also happen to need no server, account or monetization. So the on-device constraint and the gentle tone reinforce each other rather than compete. The design weakness most worth watching is weekly items: no published study compares weekly and daily streak framing, and the slow convergence of Loop's score suggests that weekly routines need their own occurrence-based display.

The decisive product choices are about trust more than features. In this niche, users judge an app's legitimacy from its name, icon, monetization and developer identity before they try a single check-in, and the copycat and "unofficial library" apps have already taught them to be suspicious. A neutral brand, an original icon, no ads or subscriptions, a plain disclaimer, links out instead of embedded content, and visibly native widgets and reminders address store review, trademark exposure and community trust at once. The next step should be a short test with a handful of practising Witness households to check the unvalidated assumptions: meeting-day prompts, the monthly ministry toggle, and the family item on a shared device.
