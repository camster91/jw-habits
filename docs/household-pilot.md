## Entry and privacy

Start after owner-approved TestFlight/Play distribution is processed and the
signed-device baseline passes. Record exact source/artifact/build identities from
[the device template](device-release-results-template.md). Re-test material fixes
on the new build; earlier observations keep their original build/date.

Cameron approves recruitment/contact and explains voluntary participation before
any session. A participant may stop, skip a task or decline recording. Use household
codes H01–H05 in review evidence. Keep contact details and any consent records
privately under Cameron's control; do not put religion, names, private notes or
backup exports in GitHub. Do not collect congregation, publisher or third-party
information. Choose fabricated content for observed tasks; participants can use
the app privately between sessions without giving the team their notes or history.

Explain that the app is independent, on-device, without account/sync, and backups
are plaintext. No analytics or screen recording is enabled for this protocol.
Obtain separate explicit consent if a later research plan proposes recording.
Cameron agrees storage location, access and deletion timing with participants before
collection; the draft invents no retention promise. Summarize only de-identified
observations approved for the intended review destination.

## First session — observe before explaining

Allow approximately 30–45 minutes, adjusting for access needs. Provide the task
prompt without the navigation answer. Record completed independently, completed
with assistance, not completed or skipped; note the point of confusion and elapsed
time if useful. Ask the participant to explain what they expected, rather than
scoring their spirituality or actual routines. Assistance ends an unassisted result;
keep the observation before helping. Do not treat an agent simulation as a household.

## Follow-up — ordinary usage observations

Agree a short observation period that includes a meeting day and its evening
before, plus a family/ministry opportunity if the participant chooses. This draft
sets no launch date or platform eligibility duration. Record the agreed dates and
actual completion, including missed days/dropouts. Do not repeatedly prompt usage
just to produce a favourable result.

Use these questions after the agreed period:

- What did you expect the meeting prompts to do, and what actually happened?
- Which prompt arrived at a useful or inconvenient time? Describe it without private details.
- How did recording ministry sharing/studies or hours fit your use? What was confusing?
- What did you think when a routine was missed or grace appeared?
- Which wording, numbers or celebrations felt supportive, uncomfortable or unnecessary?
- Which tasks would you return to? Which did you avoid, and why?
- Did you understand where your information was kept and how to protect it?
- What would prevent you from trusting or using this app?

Permission denial or missing notification delivery is a concrete technical finding,
not proof prompts feel wrong or right. Keep actual device delivery evidence separate
from a person's preference. Support needs and declined tasks remain visible rather
than counted as passes.

## Exit and decisions

#252's five criteria require real observations: meeting-day prompts, ministry/count/
pioneer hours, grace frequency, unassisted onboarding, and tone. A 3–5 household
sample tests those assumptions; it does not establish retention or representative
market success. Do not invent a satisfaction percentage or silently omit a failure.

The reviewer summarizes each criterion as supported, contradicted, mixed or untested,
with build-bound examples and the actual sample. Data loss, inaccessible core flows,
misleading activity or notification behaviour and unresolved trust/tone concerns
receive a named owner and fix/retest decision before launch recommendation.
Cameron decides accepted product tradeoffs and any explicit deferral; record the
reason and affected criterion. Publish findings/create issues only with authorization;
prepare them locally first and reuse the existing backlog when appropriate.
